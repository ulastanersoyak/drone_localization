drone_localization

How it works

Locating a drone from a pole with UWB range and a camera bearing, fused in a Kalman filter.

getting startedhow it workssource on github

The idea: a pole beside the flight area knows how far the drone is (UWB ranging between an anchor on the pole and a tag on the drone) and in which direction it is (a camera on the pole, with a neural network finding the drone in the image). Distance + direction = a 3D point, measured from the ground with no GPS needed. A Kalman filter turns those noisy, delayed points into a smooth track and can add the drone's own IMU and GPS when they are available.

the sensors

uwb range

Time of flight between anchor and tag gives a distance. It is accurate in all directions but says nothing about direction. Simulated in pole_tracking_sim from the true distance plus noise.

camera bearing

YOLO finds the drone's pixel (u, v). The pinhole model turns the pixel into a unit ray in the world, using the pan-tilt pose at the instant of the frame. Accurate across the ray, blind along it.

imu (optional)

The drone's own acceleration in ENU carries the state between pole measurements.

gps (optional)

A noisy absolute position and horizontal velocity. Useful for initialization and as a sanity anchor.

The two pole sensors complement each other: UWB is good exactly where the camera is weak (distance along the ray) and vice versa.

the detector

  • Training data without labelling. make_dataset.py saves frames from simulated flights in AirSim, in several places and weathers. For each frame it takes AirSim's own 2D box of the drone (the box Unreal computes from the drone's model, with the simulation paused so box and image match), which gives a YOLO label for free.
  • Live. yolo_ros runs our weights on the pole camera. It always takes the newest frame, so a slow model drops frames instead of falling behind, and publishes the boxes with the frame's timestamp on /pole/detections; its debug node draws them on the image.
  • Synthetic. For runs without YOLO, synthetic_detect.py projects the true position into the camera with noise, misses and a delay: the fusion alone, with a detector that is right up to its pixel noise.

the filter

x = [ x, y, z, v_x, v_y, v_z ] local ENU frame of the world

Same constant-velocity model and kalman_filter<6> core as the prediction project. IMU acceleration, when present, is the control input of the predict step. Each sensor is a separate update, and any subset works:

updatemeasureshow
range1 valuer = ‖p − pole‖. Nonlinear, so it is linearized around the current estimate (an EKF step): H is the unit vector from the pole to the estimate.
bearing2 valuesThe sideways offset of the estimate from the camera ray, along two axes perpendicular to the ray. The noise is an angle, so the allowed sideways error grows with distance.
gps3 + 2 valuesposition and horizontal velocity, linear

Every update is gated by its normalised innovation squared against a χ² threshold for its degrees of freedom, so a wrong detection (a bird, a reflection) is rejected instead of pulling the track away.

starting, recovering

  • Initialization: the first detection that has a UWB range becomes pole + r · ray, or the first GPS fix when there is one.
  • Recovery: if many detections in a row are rejected, the program trusts them over the state and re-initializes from the raw range + ray point.
  • Health: if the state or covariance ever becomes non-finite (NaN/∞), the estimator reports unhealthy and is reset rather than freezing on garbage.

delays: fusing in timestamp order

A detection arrives late. YOLO needs time, but the frame was taken earlier, and applying it as if it were current would put the drone where it used to be. So every measurement (range, detection, IMU, GPS) goes into a buffer keyed by its measurement time. The filter runs a fixed --latency behind the simulation and consumes the buffer in time order, so each measurement is applied at the moment it was taken. Detections older than that window count as “late” and are dropped.

steering the camera

A fixed camera loses the drone as soon as it leaves the field of view. The pan-tilt mount is steered toward the filter's estimate, never the ground truth, with a slew-rate limit like a real servo. That makes it a closed loop: a good estimate keeps the drone in view, which keeps the estimate good. AirSim publishes the camera pose of every image; tf2 keeps them over time, so each detection's ray uses the pose at the frame's timestamp.
